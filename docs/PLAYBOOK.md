# The Point of Failure: playbook

What we have tried, what the numbers said, and what we do because of it.
Every new video starts by reading this page, and ends by adding to it.

How to use it:
- One line per experiment: what changed, what we expected, the number that
  decides it, the result. Numbers come from YouTube Studio (screenshots) or
  the channel report; no guesses.
- Small samples: a long video has 30 to 50 viewers in its first days, so one
  person moves a retention curve by 2 to 3 points. Only big differences
  count. Write "not readable" when that is the honest answer.

## 1. Owner's standing rules

- Free, public-domain or Creative Commons material only. No TV footage, no
  licensed stock. Credits in the description.
- Facts only, honest and direct. No race, no politics.
- Never photos of the dead or of remains; never a victim's last moments.
- Nothing frozen in the 3D. The 2D is built line by line.
- No picture shown twice. Moving footage is preferred over stills.
- One Short per long video. 24 hours between posts since 10 Nov (was 48; the owner may waive it).
- The owner reviews every video before it is uploaded. Never delete and
  re-upload a public video.
- Old videos are not touched (titles included) once published.
- Plans are written down first, and analysis is based on numbers.
- No AI-generated video, ever (owner, 2026-11-05): the animation carries the
  story. The AI dramatization clip is removed from the pipeline.
- The "altered or synthetic content" question is left unanswered on upload,
  like the made-for-kids one (owner, 2026-11-05).

## 3. What works

- Real photo of the event plus a short line on the thumbnail. Teton
  ("A LEAK DID THIS.", the breach photo) got 6.5% click-through against
  1.9% for Aloha. Aloha's rotation: x-ray 8.7 views a day, photo 7.1,
  drawing 6.5, 3D model only 0.4.
- Shorts reach 1,200 to 1,700 people each, 92% of them from the Shorts feed.
- In Shorts: 2D that keeps moving and the real photo with the toll hold
  viewers (2 to 3 points lost a second). The breakup or fall as the first
  shot makes people replay (retention starts above 100%).
- In long videos: whoever reaches the disaster chapter watches to the end
  (Teton flat at 23% from 2:35).

## 4. What does not work

- Shorts: every turn from the event to an explanation is a cliff (Aloha
  6.5, Columbia about 9, Teton about 18 points a second), and the worst
  is a cut to a nearly empty dark screen with technical words. Five
  Shorts in a row stopped at 1,200 to 1,700 views: the feed's first test
  batch, then nothing. Half the viewers swipe in the first second
  (stayed to watch 52 to 54% since Aloha; Hyatt 70.5%).

- A slow 3D close-up in a Short: the worst drop every time (Aloha 6.5,
  Columbia about 9 points a second). Cut from Shorts since Teton.
- Moving the animation to 0:15 in long videos did not keep people longer:
  Teton 30.6% against Aloha 30%. No gain, no loss.
- The first 20 seconds of a long video lose a third of the viewers (Teton
  100% to 68%), in both orders. This part (preview, photo burst, title) has
  never been changed.
- Explanation with no context (the 3D establishing shot and the 2D build
  from 0:20 to 1:15 on Teton) lost another third.
- Shorts do not send viewers to the long videos: 0 subscribers from 776
  Columbia Short viewers, and the long videos get 8 to 141 views.
- Deleting and re-uploading: the Columbia re-upload got 24 impressions.
  Likely a cause, not proven.

## 6. Decided for the next long video

- Titles lead with the words people search, then the hook after a colon:
  "[Name] [Collapse/Disaster] ([year]): [hook]", under 65 characters.
  Evidence: "teton dam collapse 1976" about 36K searches a month and
  "hyatt regency walkway collapse" about 36K, yet search brought Teton only
  10% of its views.
- Order: the opening as it is (to about 0:15), then a short teaser of real
  footage or photos with the hook lines (about 15 s), then the animation
  from about 0:30. Decided by: retention at 0:30 and 1:00 against Teton
  (56% and 40%).
- Music under the animation chapters is replaced (owner: it does not fit
  the scenes). Audition on 5 Nov: 8 samples of 7 Kevin MacLeod tracks under
  the real Teton picture and voice. The owner liked none of them and picked
  "Impending Boom" (sample 6) to move on. It now plays on through both
  animation chapters (the structure, the physics). Still open: a better
  track, possibly from the YouTube Audio Library (the owner downloads it in
  Studio; this session cannot reach it).
- The 3D models get a finish (kits/look.py): dusk sky with the real sun
  position, painted and rusted steel, rippled river, valley hills, 1960s
  cars, depth of field. Tested on Silver Bridge stills on 5 Nov; a short
  moving test comes before any episode.

## 6b. Decided for the next Short (from E2)

- Frame 1 is the fall in full colour, mid-motion, with one short line in
  big type; no grey model, no small two-line header.
- No cut to a dark or half-empty screen: the 2D starts already drawn and
  shows the break happening, not the theory.
- Plain words only ("one steel link cracked"), no part names a viewer has
  to learn. Every shot 4 s or less; about 15 s in all (viewers stay 13 s
  on average).
- The last line leads back into the first frame, for replays.
- Owner: set "Related video" on each Short to its long video in Studio.
- A zoomed 2D side fills the screen down to the bottom; a part the size of
  the real thing (the 1/8-inch crack) gets a ring closing in on it rather
  than being drawn bigger than it was (first used on the Silver Bridge
  Short, E4).

What it decides, for every long video from now on:
- The disaster is on screen in the first 5 seconds, moving. No "stay to the
  end" card, no black, no title card before it (Silver Bridge lost 3 of 18
  viewers from 0:12 to 0:22, right there).
- No shot over about 8 seconds, anywhere. A 2D drawing is cut into steps
  with their own framing (wide, close on the part, the label); a photo is
  held 6 to 8 s with movement; the ending is pictures, not a text card.
- The collapse is the centrepiece: shown close, step by step, with cars
  and splashes, not one distant 12-second shot.
- A sound for each scene (rain, strain, snap, splash) as well as the music.
- A question before each answer ("Where would water collect?"), as now,
  every 30 to 60 seconds.
- Keep what they lack: the investigation's facts and real material.
- Thumbnails: curiosity, not wreckage. Sabin's shows a 1927 car next to a
  1967 car ("When cars get heavier…"); our postcard got 2.5% CTR against
  Teton's 6.5%. A video that is never clicked cannot hold anyone.
- Decided by the owner (10 Nov): three long videos a week (Mon, Wed, Fri)
  with each Short the next day, Sunday off. The upload gate is 24 h
  (src/upload_gate.py, every workflow's spacing step). New kits only where
  a disaster cannot reuse one.

## 6d. The 3D against Sabin, frame by frame (9 Nov)

The owner sent Sabin's Silver Bridge video (10:25). Compared one frame every
2 s against our published Silver Bridge (foZj6XFlEKc), 3D only (the owner:
"the 3D model animation ONLY"). Side-by-side stills: wide, joint, deck and
cars, fall, water.

| | Sabin | Ours (Silver Bridge) |
|---|---|---|
| Parts close up | eyebar, pin, cap, nut, bevelled edges, cast texture | flat boxes and discs, no bevels; the crack is a glowing slot |
| Cars | modelled 1920s to 1960s cars, wheels, glass, chrome, many on the deck | coloured boxes |
| The fall | 0:05 to 0:24 and 5:20 to 5:50: deck hinges off the tower, cars tumble out, tower tips, white splashes, 6+ angles | 3:34 to 3:48: two distant shots, parts slide flat onto a mirror river, no splash, cars do not fall |
| Water | waves, foam, splashes, underwater shots | a mirror |
| Land | green banks, trees, city, clouds | sand-coloured ramps, empty |
| Cameras | 4 to 6 s, low under the deck, at the joint, following falling cars | about 6 s at the start, then an 8 s static close-up and a 14 s distant fall |
| Light | bright, saturated, clear | dusk, flat, dark |
| Where we are ahead | | the facts (the investigation's numbers, real places) |

What Sabin's does that we did not have, as code: physics-looking falls
(gravity, tumbling, hinging), splashes, modelled cars, detail on every close
part, more angles. Their render is not photoreal (clean, game-like); the
gap is motion and detail, not the renderer.

The Tacoma Narrows model (src/kits/narrows_3d.py, started 9 Nov) is built to
close that gap, part by part:
- Steel: riveted plate (rivet rows in the bump), bevelled edges, stiffeners
  on the 8 ft plate girders, cable bands with bolts, wire-rope texture. New
  paint, no rust (the bridge was four months old).
- Motion: one geometry-nodes group moves deck, hangers and cables together
  from two numbers a frame (twist, heave) through the bridge's mode shapes:
  the deck rolls about its axis, hangers lean and stay straight, cables rise
  and fall. No per-part hand keys.
- The fall: the deck tears into pieces of 100 to 200 ft, each keeps its
  twisted shape, hinges down from the edge still holding, lets go and falls
  under real gravity (about 3.5 s from 195 ft), tilted, and goes under.
- Water: Puget Sound in a 42 mph wind (crossed wave bands, chop, whitecaps);
  each piece throws a splash where it lands: sheets of water up to about
  120 ft, mist that hangs for 6 s, a foam ring. Worked out from the time
  since the hit (no simulation), so any frame renders alone, in any shard.
- World: steep bluffs under fir forest (about 100,000 instanced trees), an
  overcast November sky.
- The abandoned car: a late-1930s sedan (rounded body, fenders, running
  boards, chrome, glass) in place of box cars.
- Matched to the 1940 film (Barney Elliott's, Prelinger Archives,
  dedicated to the public domain; on preview-media as
  sources/tacoma/film_1940.mp4), 10 Nov: towers as plain portals (legs and
  solid plate struts, no X bracing); one solid white centre line;
  Coatsworth's car pale and rounded, against the curb; the Rapid Transfer
  Company's delivery truck on its side beside it; the splash a white cloud
  that rolls up past the deck and drifts downwind; afterwards the side spans
  sag, snapped hangers dangle from the cables, and the last piece by each
  tower hangs from it down toward the water.
- Owner, 10 Nov: the car and the truck fall with the deck (shown, like
  Sabin's falling cars); the Tacoma long video goes out Friday 13 Nov, the
  review copy Thursday; nothing on Wednesday.
- Local renders: Blender 5.2 (pip bpy) runs in the session; half-size
  frames take 8 to 15 s at 12 to 16 samples on 4 cores.
